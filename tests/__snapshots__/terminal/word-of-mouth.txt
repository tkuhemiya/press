
  Why growth slows down on its own
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  A product that spreads by word of mouth grows faster the bigger it gets, at
  first. Then it slows down, even though nothing about the product changed. Two
  feedback loops explain both halves of that story, and the point where one
  overtakes the other.

  ① More customers means more people telling their friends.
  ② Those friends become customers, and the loop goes round again.
  ③ But every new customer is one fewer person left to convince.
  ④ A saturated market turns fewer referrals into sign-ups.
  R1 Word of mouth (reinforcing): Customers +→ Referrals +→ Customers
  B1 Saturation (balancing): Customers +→ Market saturation −→ Referrals +→
     Customers

  Word of mouth[a] is a reinforcing loop: every link is +, so growth feeds on
  itself. Saturation closes a balancing loop through market saturation[b]: it
  has one - link, so it pushes back harder the bigger the customer base gets.

  Early on, saturation is tiny and the reinforcing loop wins. Later the
  balancing loop catches up, and growth flattens into an S-curve. A price change
  acts on customers only after a delay, which is why its effect is easy to
  misread.
